One OMOP warehouse from two very different EHR exports

Cedar Valley Health sends an Epic Clarity extract; Riverside Community Health sends a FHIR R4 bulk export. A PySpark ETL maps both to the OMOP Common Data Model v5.4, OHDSI-style checks test the result, and a new-user cohort study runs on top.

Synthetic data only: two fictional health systems, no real patients.

What each site actually sent

Local codes: assisted suggestions, human decisions

For each unmapped local code, candidate standard concepts are retrieved from the vocabulary, the closest match is suggested, deterministic checks validate it, and a person approves, corrects or rejects it. Only approved rows reach the ETL.

One source code, several standard concepts

Data-quality checks

Modeled on OHDSI's Data Quality Dashboard and the Kahn framework. A check fails when its violation rate is above its threshold; a check that passes but still found rows is passed with flags. Fatal checks (duplicate keys, orphan rows) stop the pipeline before analysis.

Two sites, one model

After harmonisation the two health systems should look alike wherever their patients do. Units are the classic trap: Epic stores weight in ounces, FHIR sends kilograms or pounds, and a few HbA1c results arrive in IFCC mmol/mol.
